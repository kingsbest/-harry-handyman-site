Where the rules stand in 2026

The Duties That Cost Money If You Miss Them

A plain summary of what a private landlord in England has to keep on top of, and where we fit. Not legal advice — see the note at the bottom.

Gas safety — every 12 months

Every gas appliance and flue checked annually by a Gas Safe registered engineer, with a record given to the tenant. Breaches can lead to prosecution as well as a fine.

We book the engineer and chase the certificate.

Electrical — EICR every 5 years

Fixed wiring inspected at least every five years, a copy to the tenant, and any C1, C2 or FI findings remedied within 28 days. Councils can impose penalties of up to £30,000 per breach.

We arrange the EICR and quote the remedials.

Smoke & carbon monoxide alarms

A smoke alarm on every storey, a CO alarm in every room with a fixed combustion appliance, all tested at the start of a tenancy and repaired or replaced when a tenant reports a fault.

We fit, test and record them.

Repairs, damp and mould

The structure, exterior, heating, hot water, sanitary fittings and installations for gas, water and electricity are the landlord's to keep in repair. Hazards including damp and mould must be dealt with, not explained away as tenant lifestyle.

This is most of what we do.

Awaab's Law in the private sector

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 extends Awaab's Law principles to privately rented homes: serious hazards investigated and put right to fixed timescales rather than at the landlord's convenience. Timescales are being phased in by regulation.

Fast attendance and dated photo evidence.

Section 21 has gone

From 1 May 2026, no-fault Section 21 evictions ended in England and tenancies became periodic. Possession now runs through the statutory grounds — which makes a well-maintained property and a good paper trail worth considerably more than it used to be.

Records after every visit, kept for your file.

Decent Homes Standard

A minimum standard for privately rented homes is being introduced under the Act, covering condition, warmth, safety and reasonably modern kitchens and bathrooms. Worth knowing before your next void.

We'll flag what a property is likely to need.

Licensing & HMO conditions

Mandatory, additional and selective licensing all vary by borough — Greenwich, Lewisham and Southwark do not run identical schemes. Licence conditions usually carry their own schedule of works.

Send us the schedule and we'll price it.

Please note — this is a general summary for England, current at the time of writing, and is not legal advice. Penalty levels, commencement dates and licensing rules change and vary by borough. Check the position with your local authority, a solicitor or a landlord association before you rely on it. We're tradespeople: we do the works and the evidence, not the legal opinion.

Landlord services — frequently asked

Do you work for landlords with one property or only portfolios?

Both. Plenty of our landlord work is a single flat or a house someone inherited or moved out of. You get the same named contact and the same flat prices whether it's one door or twenty.

Can you deal with the tenant directly?

Yes, if you want us to. With your written go-ahead we arrange access with the tenant, confirm the appointment, attend, and report back to you with photos. You're not the middleman on every text message.

Do you issue gas and electrical certificates yourselves?

No. We don't carry out gas work or notifiable electrical work in-house. We work with Gas Safe registered engineers and Part P registered electricians, book them in and hand you the certificate. Their fee is shown separately on your quote from our labour.

What does a void turnaround include?

Whatever the property needs between tenancies: repairs from the check-out report, touch-up or full redecoration, sealant and grout, door and window adjustments, appliance checks and a clean handover. We quote the whole turnaround as one flat price after a walk-round, so you know the cost before the void starts costing you rent.

Can you do HMO works?

Yes. Fire doors, door closers, signage, emergency lighting and fire alarm testing, communal repairs and general wear and tear. Where a licence condition requires a specific certificate or a specialist sign-off we coordinate the right registered contractor rather than pretending it's ours to sign.

How do you handle emergencies out of hours?

Call the London line. Genuine emergencies — no heat, no water, no security, an active leak — get a same-day response where we can, and we make the property safe first and quote any follow-up work separately.

Do you provide photo reports for our records?

Yes, as standard on landlord work. Before and after photos and a short written note of what was done, sent the same day. It's the paperwork that saves the argument later — with tenants, agents or a council inspector.

Can you work alongside my letting agent?

Yes. We work either directly for landlords or as the maintenance contractor an agent instructs. Tell us who's authorising and paying at the quote stage and we keep the reporting going to the right person.

Are your crew insured and DBS-checked for tenanted properties?

Yes. Every crew member is DBS-checked, certificates available on request, and we're insured for domestic and commercial work. That matters when the person answering the door is your tenant rather than you.

Is a maintenance contract cheaper than calling you job by job?

Usually, yes, if you have more than a couple of properties or a building with communal areas. A scheduled visit catches small faults before they become emergency call-outs, and it spreads the cost into a predictable monthly figure. Ask us to price both ways and pick the cheaper one.
